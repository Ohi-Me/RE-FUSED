==============================================================================
  U15.2 — CONCLUSION (paper-ready)
==============================================================================

We re-examined day-ahead forecasting on the RE-FUSED-Alpha panel (18 Indian
states, 47,286 state-day observations, chronological test period
2024-01-01 to 2025-04-22) using an isolated pipeline with a mechanical
leakage proof: every observation from the test boundary onward was replaced
with noise and the pre-boundary features verified bit-identical across
18 target-horizon configurations and 712,152 row-comparisons.

The published PatchTST+MCAG result (28.20% average MAPE, MASE 3.4748) is
reproducible, and its weakness is attributable to the input specification
rather than the architecture: the original feature list contains no lag of
total_generation_mwh, so the model was asked to predict the level of a
series it was never shown. Two further measurement issues were identified —
the published model MASE was computed with pooled scaling while its
baselines were macro-averaged across states, and the validation split was a
state holdout rather than a time holdout.

Persistence is a strong benchmark on this panel (MASE 0.4163),
beating ARIMA, ETS, seasonal-naive and rolling-mean alternatives. Market
price in particular is near-trivially persistent, which the original audit
attributes to the 87.3% imputation rate smoothing the series.

The strongest model found is Hybrid_Persistence_XGB, at MASE
0.3841 against persistence at 0.4163 — an improvement of
7.75%. It wins in 17 of 18 states.
The gain comes from two corrections rather than from model capacity:
forecasting the residual from a persistence base (which removes the
train-to-test level shift) and normalising each state's target by its own
MASE denominator (which aligns the training loss with the macro-averaged
metric on a panel spanning three orders of magnitude).

Statistically: Diebold-Mariano tests do NOT consistently reject equal
accuracy against persistence at the 5% level, and the moving-block
bootstrap interval for the error difference includes zero.
The improvement survives re-seeding: the 95% confidence interval over
8 seeds lies entirely below the persistence benchmark.

Limitations. (i) 87.3% of state-day rows carry at least one imputed value;
the smoothing this induces is the most likely reason persistence is so hard
to beat, and it caps what any model can demonstrate here. (ii) The mean
market price roughly doubles between train and test, so 85.6% of test rows
sit above the per-state training 90th percentile — a covariate shift no
fixed-range learner handles well. (iii) The test period contains a 54-day
data outage (2024-12-11 to 2025-02-02) which removes window-based models'
coverage immediately afterwards. (iv) Horizons are limited to 1-3 days to
match the original PRED=3 setting; longer horizons, where persistence decays
fastest, are where a learned model would most plausibly win and are left to
future work. (v) No time-series foundation model was evaluated, because
installing one would have altered the pinned torch build that produced the
Part I results.

Reporting note: MAPE is a percentage error and is never restated as an
accuracy. A 28.20% MAPE does not correspond to 71.8% accuracy.
